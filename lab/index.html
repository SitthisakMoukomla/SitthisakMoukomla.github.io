Mission

We turn satellite radar, LiDAR, and machine learning into measurements that matter — forest carbon that can be verified, urban heat that can be mitigated, and freshwater ecosystems that can be protected.

The Research Unit in Geospatial Research and Analytics for Climate and Environment (GRACE Lab) is a university research group in the Department of Geography, Faculty of Liberal Arts, Thammasat University. The unit develops Earth-observation methods for climate and environmental applications in tropical Southeast Asia, spanning synthetic aperture radar (SAR) and LiDAR analysis, surface energy balance, statistical and machine-learning modeling, and large-scale geospatial computing on cloud platforms. Our work supports measurement, reporting, and verification (MRV) of forest carbon, climate-resilient urban planning, and national Earth-observation capacity in Thailand and the region.

Research Programs

01 · Forests

Forest Carbon & MRV

Wall-to-wall aboveground biomass and individual-tree carbon for tropical forests, built from SAR time series, spaceborne LiDAR, and close-range laser scanning — designed to meet the standards of carbon measurement, reporting, and verification.

  • SLAM and UAV-LiDAR co-registration for individual-tree carbon (Sakaerat Biosphere Reserve)
  • Sentinel-1, ALOS PALSAR-2, GEDI, and ICESat-2 fusion for national-scale biomass mapping
  • Mangrove blue-carbon inventory with handheld SLAM LiDAR for Thailand's T-VER carbon program
02 · Cities

Urban Climate & Heat

How tropical megacities heat up — and what satellite archives, machine learning, and foundation-model embeddings can tell us about mitigating it.

  • Decadal dynamics of day and nighttime surface urban heat islands in the Bangkok Metropolitan Region
  • Impervious-surface expansion across Southeast Asian megacities using foundation-model embeddings
  • Machine-learning downscaling of land-surface temperature for heterogeneous urban environments
03 · Water

Water & Ecosystems

Linking satellite observations with field measurements to track water quality, soil moisture, and ecosystem condition across Thailand's agricultural and freshwater landscapes.

  • Nutrient and chlorophyll-a dynamics in tropical freshwater ecosystems from Sentinel-2
  • Soil-moisture retrieval from Sentinel-1 time series over cropland
  • Surface energy balance and eddy-covariance flux measurement in tropical forests
04 · Methods

Geospatial AI & Cloud Computing

The computational backbone of the unit: applied machine learning, remote-sensing foundation models, and petabyte-scale analysis on cloud platforms — plus training the next generation of geospatial data scientists.

  • Foundation models and embeddings for Earth observation
  • Google Earth Engine and Microsoft Planetary Computer workflows at national scale
  • Open teaching in programming, geographic data science, and AI for geographers

Principal Investigator

Principal Investigator & Head of Unit

Sitthisak Moukomla, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor of Geography, Faculty of Liberal Arts, Thammasat University

Dr. Moukomla holds a Ph.D. in Geography from the University of Colorado Boulder with a Graduate Certificate in Remote Sensing, and spent two decades as a geo-informatics scientist at GISTDA, Thailand's national space agency, before joining Thammasat University. His research spans SAR and LiDAR analysis for forest carbon, surface energy balance, urban thermal environments, and applied machine learning for Earth observation. He has served in international calibration-validation and space-technology programs including CEOS working groups and ASEAN SCOSA, and holds validation and verification (VVB) training credentials from Thailand's Greenhouse Gas Management Organization (TGO).
